Detailed Description

Solvent-accessible surface area of the atoms with the LCPO method (Weiser, Shenkin, Still, J.

Comput. Chem. 20, 217 (1999)), for proteins, nucleic acids and glycans.

The parameters of each atom are assigned from its atom and residue names. Atoms of monoatomic ions and hydrogens have no parameters and are skipped. The neighbours are found with a cell list and kept in a Verlet list, which is rebuilt only when an atom has moved by more than about half of its skin; the result is the same as with an all-pairs search.

Assign the parameters of the atoms.

For atom i: atomNames[i] and residueNames[i] are its names; glycan[i] tells whether it belongs to a monosaccharide and bridge[i] whether it is an oxygen (or a protein atom) that carries a glycosidic bond. The N-terminal nitrogen and the C-terminal oxygen are the first and the last atoms that are neither in a glycan nor in an ion.

std::vector< double > & area )

The areas (Angstrom^2) of the atoms with an area, from the positions (nm) of all the atoms.

Only the atoms with an area of index first, first+stride, ... in the list of the atoms with an area are computed (to split the work over MPI ranks); area is resized to the number of atoms and is zero for the others. The neighbour list is updated first.

The parameters of the atoms (radius in Angstrom, including the probe radius of 1.4 Angstrom, and the four LCPO coefficients), keyed by "RESIDUE_ATOM".

Glycan oxygens have a second entry with the suffix "_L", used when they carry a glycosidic bond.
